Platform Comparison

Orchen vs Flint

Both give independent schools AI that students use for learning. Flint is built around the activity a teacher assigns. Orchen is built around the student and the adults who support them.

Updated November 10, 2026

If your school wants teachers to build and assign AI activities, watch sessions live, grade against rubrics, and roster from your SIS or LMS, Flint is a mature platform for that workflow. Teachers can start on its free plan, and many independent schools already use it.

Orchen is in its founding-school year, and each school works directly with the founder. It starts from the student: guided work grounded in class materials, a picture of each student that teachers, advisors, and parents see through their own view, and source conversations deleted within seven days by default.

Honest Assessment

Where Flint is strong

These are genuine strengths, not soft-pedaled. If any of these are your primary need, Flint deserves serious consideration.

  • Free for teachers, with no student limit

    Any teacher can start today. Flint’s free plan covers teachers and their students, with up to 3 teachers and admins per workspace, and includes chats, activities, and class analytics. Flint pricing

  • Live sessions and alerts

    Teachers watch a sessions table update as students work. Sparky flags repeated wrong answers, signs of distress, disengagement, or a student asking for the teacher, and each alert opens the session at the message that triggered it. Sessions and monitoring

  • Rubric grading, a gradebook, and standards dashboards

    Teachers build rubrics and Sparky grades submitted sessions against them, with teacher override. Each class has a gradebook with weighted categories and CSV download. Schools can add standards from presets such as Common Core, NGSS, and AP and track coverage. Grading and analytics

  • Fits the systems a school already runs

    Google and Microsoft sign-in, SIS and LMS rostering from Blackbaud, ClassLink, Schoology, and Canvas, plus access hours and allowed networks set by the school. School setup

  • Scale, references, and training

    Flint reports more than 250,000 teachers on the platform and publishes teacher testimonials and case studies from independent schools. School licenses include professional development sessions for faculty, and its security page lists signed data agreements with schools in New York and other states. How Orchen Approaches This

Where Orchen is different

Not better in every case. Different in ways that matter for specific schools.

  • Built around the student, not the activity

    Flint organizes work around the activity a teacher assigns, and it also builds a weekly learning profile for each student. In Orchen that picture of the student is the product. It carries across subjects and teachers, and each adult who supports the student sees it through their own view.

  • Parents get their own window

    Flint says there is no parent login. Orchen gives parents a weekly digest in plain language: what their child worked on, bright spots, and one thing to try at home. Five visibility tiers let the school set the default and the ceiling.

  • Advisors get a weekly narrative

    Orchen writes a structured weekly narrative for each advisee, covering independent effort, curiosity, and any concerning patterns, alongside the teacher’s class and student insight.

  • The school decides who reads conversations, and sources are deleted

    In Flint, teachers can open sessions in their activities and school admins can open any session or chat. In Orchen the school sets what each role can see, staff access is logged, and source conversations are deleted within 7 days by default. The derived insight is retained separately.

  • Questions first is part of the design

    In Flint, teachers set how much help Sparky gives for each activity, which suits schools that want that flexibility. In Orchen the Socratic approach is built into the architecture rather than configured per activity.

  • Set up with the founder, in person

    Founding schools work directly with Emil on configuration, in-person training, and support through the pilot, and their teachers help shape what gets built next.

Decision Guide

How to decide

Choose Flint if…

  • Your teachers want to build and assign AI activities, and start free today
  • Live session monitoring, rubric auto-grading, and a built-in gradebook are priorities
  • You need SIS or LMS rostering and Google or Microsoft sign-in from day one
  • You want a platform with many independent schools’ references behind it

Choose Orchen if…

  • You want parents and advisors, not only teachers, to have their own view of each student’s learning
  • Your board or counsel wants source conversations deleted on a short, defined schedule
  • You want the questions-first approach built in rather than set activity by activity
  • You want to work directly with the founder on setup, training, and what gets built next

Consider both if…

  • Your teachers already use Flint’s free plan for activities. A single-class Orchen pilot can run alongside it, so your team can compare what each one tells you about the same students.
Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can parents see their child’s learning in Flint?

Flint says there is no parent login. A student can show a parent their own sessions, and the school can export a student’s data if a parent asks. Read Flint’s parent page.

Orchen gives parents a weekly digest at the visibility level your school sets, with five tiers from summary-only upward.

Who can read student conversations in Flint and in Orchen?

Flint says teachers can open any student’s session in their activities, and school admins can open any session or chat in the school, including students’ personal chats. See sessions and monitoring.

In Orchen your school controls what teachers, advisors, and parents can see, including whether they can open conversations. Staff access is scoped to role and logged, and source conversations are deleted within seven days by default.

Does Flint delete student conversation data?

Flint says student data is never used to train AI models and that admins can request an export of a student’s data. Its security page does not state a retention window for conversations, so ask Flint for the retention and deletion terms in your school’s agreement. Read Flint’s security page.

Orchen deletes source conversation transcripts within seven days by default. Derived learning profiles and narratives follow the school’s retention policy. Read Orchen’s retention policy.

Does Flint build a profile of each student?

Yes. Flint documents a learning profile for each student, with strengths and areas of improvement, refreshed weekly from sessions across classes. See grading and analytics.

Orchen also builds a learning profile. The difference is who it serves: teachers, advisors, and parents each see it through their own view, with access set by the school.

Does Orchen have live session monitoring and a gradebook?

Flint documents a live sessions table, rubric auto-grading, and a class gradebook with weighted categories. If those are your first requirement, Flint is built for that workflow. Sessions · Grading.

Orchen takes a different approach. Teachers review submissions, active time, exchanges, and the assignment transcript, then generate a learning summary. Quizzes are graded semantically, and the Writing Studio checks drafts against a rubric.

Can we pilot Orchen alongside Flint?

Yes. You can evaluate Orchen alongside Flint with a free, six-week single-class pilot. Your school can keep its existing tools while testing Orchen’s guided learning and teacher, advisor, and parent insight.

Arrange a pilot with Emil. We agree on the scope before students begin, and your school reviews and finalizes its student data terms with us before any student logs in.
